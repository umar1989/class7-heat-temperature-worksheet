1 · How to Write a Full-Marks Answer

Teachers give marks for steps and keywords, not only the final answer. A correct answer with no working can lose half the marks.

A. Numerical problems (temperature conversions, faulty thermometers) — 3–4 marks

1 · GivenWrite the known value with its scale and unit (°C, °F, K).
2 · FormulaWrite the formula in symbols first.
3 · SubstitutePut in the numbers, one line at a time.
4 · AnswerFinal value + unit/scale. Underline it.

2 · Learn These (Key Points)

TermExam-ready answer
HeatA form of energy that gives us the sensation of hotness or coldness. SI unit: joule (J). CGS unit: calorie (cal). 1 cal = 4.18 J; 1 kcal = 4180 J.
TemperatureThe degree of hotness or coldness of an object. SI unit: kelvin (K); commonly measured in °C.
Direction of heat flowHeat always flows from a body at higher temperature to a body at lower temperature until equilibrium is reached. At equilibrium there is no flow of heat.
Rate of heat flowDepends on the difference in temperature of the two bodies.
Principle of calorimetryHeat energy lost by the hot body = heat energy gained by the cold body.
Effects of heatChange in temperature · change in dimensions · change in state · chemical change.
ThermometerA device used to measure temperature. It measures temperature, not heat. Principle: liquids expand when heated and contract when cooled.
Lower fixed point (LFP)Melting point of pure ice at normal atmospheric pressure (76 cm of mercury).
Upper fixed point (UFP)Boiling point of pure water at normal atmospheric pressure.
ConductionTransfer of energy from one particle to another, the particles being in direct contact. Mainly in solids (best in metals).
ConvectionMovement of fluid molecules (liquids and gases) from higher temperature regions to lower temperature regions. Hot, less dense fluid rises; cool, dense fluid sinks.
RadiationTransfer of heat by electromagnetic waves. No medium needed, so it can occur through a vacuum.

Parts of a thermometer

Glass bulb (holds mercury/coloured alcohol) → narrow capillary tube (so even a small change moves the liquid) → stem with the scale (°C or °F). It is sealed so liquid does not spill and air does not enter. Always read it at eye level.

Why mercury? Why alcohol?

Mercury
Needs very little heat to expand
Boils at 357 °C, freezes at −39 °C (wide range)
Does not stick to glass
Opaque and shiny, easy to see; expands uniformly
Alcohol
Freezing point below −100 °C (good for very cold places)
Expands more than mercury for the same change
Can be brightly coloured and easily seen
Safer than toxic mercury

Types of thermometers

TypeKey facts
ClinicalMeasures human body temperature. Range 35–42 °C (95–108 °F). Has a kink/constriction so mercury does not fall back after use. Normal body temperature 36.9 °C = 98.4 °F.
LaboratoryMeasures liquids and other substances. Range −10 °C to 110 °C. No kink, so not for body temperature.
Six's max–minRecords the highest and lowest temperature of a day (weather stations). Invented by James Six.
DigitalElectronic sensor, numerical display, no mercury. Safe, quick, easy to read.
ThermistorUses a resistor whose electrical resistance changes with temperature.

States of matter (for reasons): Solids – fixed shape and volume, particles tightly packed, only vibrate. Liquids – fixed volume, no fixed shape, particles slide over each other. Gases – no fixed shape or volume, particles far apart and move freely.
Forced vs natural convection: natural – caused only by differences in temperature (warm air rising near a radiator). Forced – uses a fan or pump (water pumped in a radiator).

4 · Model Answers (Study How Marks Are Earned)

Model 1 — °C to °F (3 marks)

Q. Convert −10 °C into the Fahrenheit scale.

Given: C = −10 °C (1)
Formula: F = (9/5)C + 32 (1)
F = (9/5)(−10) + 32 = −18 + 32 = 14 °F (1)

Model 2 — Show −40° is the same on both scales (4 marks)

Let C = F = x. (1)
Formula: C/5 = (F − 32)/9 (1)
x/5 = (x − 32)/9 ⇒ 9x = 5x − 160 ⇒ 4x = −160 (1)
x = −40. So −40 °C = −40 °F. (1)

Model 3 — Faulty thermometer (4 marks)

Q. A faulty thermometer has its fixed points marked as 10° and 110°. It reads 60° for a body. Find the correct temperature on the Celsius scale.

LFP = 10, UFP = 110, reading = 60 (1)
Correct °C = (Reading − LFP)/(UFP − LFP) × 100 (1)
= (60 − 10)/(110 − 10) × 100 = 50/100 × 100 (1)
= 50 °C (1)

Model 4 — Where do Kelvin and Fahrenheit readings match? (4 marks)

Let K = F = x. (K − 273)/100 = (F − 32)/180 (1)
180(x − 273) = 100(x − 32) (1)
180x − 49140 = 100x − 3200 ⇒ 80x = 45940 (1)
x = 574.25, so about 574 K = 574 °F (1)

Model 5 — Opinion question (3 marks)

Q. Which thermometer is better for schools: mercury, alcohol or digital? Why?

Digital thermometers are better for schools (1) because they are safe (no toxic mercury, nothing to spill if broken), (1) easy to read and give quick results. (1) Mercury ones are accurate but risky if broken.

Model 6 — “Why” answer (3 marks)

Q. Why should a laboratory thermometer not be used to measure body temperature?

A laboratory thermometer has no kink (constriction) in its tube. (1) In a clinical thermometer the kink stops the mercury from falling back quickly once removed from the body, so the reading stays steady. (1) Without it, the mercury falls at once and the reading is inaccurate. (1)

Part B answers
B1. Heat is a form of energy that gives the sensation of hotness or coldness. Temperature is the degree of hotness or coldness of an object.
B2. Heat: a form of energy; unit joule/calorie; cause of hot/cold sensation. Temperature: degree of hotness/coldness; unit kelvin/°C; measured by a thermometer.
B3. (i) Heat flows from higher to lower temperature until equilibrium. (ii) At equilibrium, heat does not flow. (iii) The rate of flow depends on the temperature difference. (iv) Heat lost by hot body = heat gained by cold body.
B4. Principle: liquids expand when heated and contract when cooled (thermal expansion). Parts: glass bulb with mercury/coloured alcohol, narrow capillary tube, stem with scale (°C/°F).
B5. Any three: needs little heat to expand; boils at 357 °C so measures high temperatures; freezes at −39 °C; does not stick to the glass; opaque, shiny and expands uniformly.
B6. Freezing point below −100 °C; expands more than mercury for the same change, so more accurate; can be coloured and seen easily; safer than mercury.
B7. Clinical: body temperature, range 35–42 °C, has a kink. Laboratory: other substances, range −10 to 110 °C, no kink.
B8. LFP: melting point of pure ice at normal atmospheric pressure (0 °C). UFP: boiling point of pure water at normal atmospheric pressure (100 °C).
B9. Conduction: transfer of energy between particles in direct contact (ironing clothes). Convection: movement of fluid molecules from higher to lower temperature regions (boiling water). Radiation: heat transfer by electromagnetic waves without a medium (Sun's heat).
B10. Conductors allow heat to pass easily (copper, iron); insulators do not (wood, plastic). Conductors have closely packed particles / free electrons; insulators have loosely arranged particles.
B11. Heat energy given out by the hot body = heat energy absorbed by the cold body.
B12. Change in temperature; change in dimensions; change in state; chemical change.

Part E answers
E1. Metals are good conductors, so heat passes quickly to the food. Plastic/wood are insulators, so they do not pass heat and the handle can be held safely.
E2. By radiation. Heat reaches the person as electromagnetic waves, not through direct contact or air movement.
E3. Black surfaces absorb more radiation from the Sun, so more heat energy is gained and we feel hotter.
E4. It reduces conduction, convection and radiation, so heat cannot easily escape from the liquid (or enter it).
E5. Wool is a heat insulator. It reduces heat loss from our body to the cold surroundings, keeping us warm.
E6. The left finger feels the water warm and the right finger feels it cold, though both are in the same water. Conclusion: we cannot judge hotness or coldness correctly by touch; a thermometer is needed.
E7. The mercury contracts when it cools, so the thread falls and the thermometer shows a lower temperature as it cools.
E8. Land heats up, and the air above it gets hot and expands, so it becomes less dense and rises. This is convection; cooler air moves in to take its place.
E9. Appreciate the question first. Mercury expands uniformly, does not stick to glass and gives accurate readings. But it is toxic, so many places prefer digital or alcohol thermometers; handle it carefully and teach safe laboratory practice.
